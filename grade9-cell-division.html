GRADE 9 · BIOLOGY

Cell Division: Mitosis and Meiosis

Explore how cells divide. Step through the stages of mitosis and meiosis, track chromosome behaviour and compare the cells produced by each process.

1. Learning objectives

  • Describe why cells divide.
  • Identify the main stages of mitosis and meiosis.
  • Explain how chromosomes are copied and separated.
  • Compare the number and genetic similarity of daughter cells.
  • Explain the role of meiosis in sexual reproduction and genetic variation.

Key terms

Chromosome: a DNA-containing structure. Chromatid: one of two copies of a replicated chromosome. Diploid: two sets of chromosomes. Haploid: one set.

4. Compare mitosis and meiosis

Mitosis

  • One nuclear division.
  • Produces two daughter cells.
  • Usually maintains chromosome number.
  • Daughter cells are genetically very similar to the parent cell, barring mutations.
  • Important for growth, repair and asexual reproduction in some organisms.

Meiosis

  • Two successive divisions: meiosis I and meiosis II.
  • Produces four haploid cells in the typical model.
  • Reduces chromosome sets from diploid to haploid.
  • Crossing over and independent assortment contribute to genetic variation.
  • Produces cells used in sexual reproduction.

In animals, meiosis produces haploid cells; in humans, the process of gamete formation includes additional developmental steps, especially in egg formation.
