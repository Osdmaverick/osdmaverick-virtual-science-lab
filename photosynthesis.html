GRADE 7 · BIOLOGY

Photosynthesis and Plant Growth

Investigate how plants use light energy to make glucose. Change the conditions available to a model plant and explore how light intensity, carbon dioxide and water affect the rate of photosynthesis.

1. Learning objectives

  • Describe photosynthesis as the process by which green plants make glucose.
  • Identify light, carbon dioxide and water as essential inputs.
  • Explain the role of chlorophyll in absorbing light energy.
  • Interpret how changing environmental conditions affects a model photosynthesis rate.
  • Use observations to identify a limiting factor.

4. Understand the results

Light intensity

Light supplies the energy needed for photosynthesis. At low light, increasing light can increase the rate. At high light, another factor may become limiting.

Carbon dioxide

Carbon dioxide is a raw material used to build glucose. When it is scarce, photosynthesis may be limited even when light is plentiful.

Water

Water is a reactant in photosynthesis. A shortage can reduce photosynthesis and cause a plant to close its stomata.

Temperature

Photosynthesis involves enzyme-controlled reactions. Very low or high temperatures can reduce the rate; the optimum varies by plant species and conditions.

Describe the conditions you tested, identify the limiting factor and explain the evidence from your observations.
