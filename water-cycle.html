The Water Cycle

Follow water as it moves between Earth's surface and the atmosphere. Investigate how sunlight, temperature and wind influence evaporation, condensation and precipitation.

1. Learning objectives

  • Describe the main stages of the water cycle.
  • Explain how solar energy drives evaporation.
  • Describe how cooling water vapour forms clouds through condensation.
  • Explain precipitation and collection in Earth's water systems.
  • Use model observations to explain how changing conditions can affect the cycle.

Key idea

Water continuously moves through the environment. Energy from the Sun drives evaporation, while cooling allows water vapour to condense into tiny droplets or ice crystals.

4. Explore the four main stages

☀️ Evaporation

Energy from sunlight warms surface water. Some liquid water changes into water vapour and enters the atmosphere.

☁️ Condensation

Water vapour cools and changes into tiny liquid droplets or ice crystals, forming clouds.

🌧️ Precipitation

Water falls from clouds to Earth's surface as rain, snow, sleet or hail.

🌊 Collection

Water gathers in rivers, lakes, oceans and underground stores. It can flow over land or soak into soil.

Transpiration from plants also adds water vapour to the atmosphere. Runoff, infiltration and groundwater movement are important parts of the wider water cycle.
